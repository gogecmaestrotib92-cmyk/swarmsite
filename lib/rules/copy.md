# Copy rules (merged: taste-skill copy.md + Vercel writing-guidelines + our DNA)

Decided by the art director 2026-11-06: **hybrid H1**, one-section-per-screen rhythm ON.

## Hero (first screen)
1. H1 = a claim with a stance, ≤ 6 words, no question, no exclamation. ("Never take a bad number again.")
2. Directly under/above it a **category + buyer + one hard fact** label: "Odds analytics for sports bettors · 28,139 analyses".
   The hard fact is a number, price, date or place — never an adjective.
3. Sub-line 15–30 words: how it works + main benefit. One sentence, two at most.
4. Exactly 2 actions in the hero: main = verb + what you get ("Check my next bet — free"), second = "See the public record".
   The main action reads identically in nav, hero, ending. Never "Learn more / Discover / Explore".

## Sections
5. Every h2 has a noun that names a thing (the record, prices, plans, questions). 1–6 words. Never a command, a question, a colon heading, or a line that only makes sense after the page.
6. Under a heading: one line to two sentences, 10–40 words. Card body ≤ 25 words with one concrete detail.
7. Use concise wording as a layout guide, not a reason to erase useful content. If a section is dense, split it or group related items while keeping every distinct offering, material fact and action. Never drop a card, an FAQ answer or a real fact solely to meet a word count.
8. One fact, one wording. A price, a count or a date appears once per page with one value (the audit's price conflict is a report item, never visible copy).
9. Numbers sit in sentences with their unit, uneven where real (28,139 not 28k; 43% not "almost half").
10. No invented proof: no quotes, reviews, counts, logos, awards that the client's site doesn't carry.

## Banned
metaphors, "where X meets Y", slogans/greetings/boasts in the hero, questions as headlines (FAQ entries excepted), exclamation marks, invented capitalised concepts, filler: seamless, effortless, cutting-edge, unlock, elevate, supercharge, reimagine, next-generation, game-changing, powerful, robust, easy, simple, quick, very, just, really; weasel: significantly, many, often, typically, generally.
AI tells: recap openers ("Now that…"), choppy three-fragment runs, spec-sheet voice ("provides", "is configurable"), personified artifacts.

## Our signature we keep (conflicts with taste-skill, decided to keep)
- Mono uppercase labels (11px, ■ bullet, numbered 01–06) — the editorial register of the winners.
- The ending: giant wordmark + "Next: …" line + one legal row. No link dump (≤ 12 links total).
